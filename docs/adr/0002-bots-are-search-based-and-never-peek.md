# Bots are search-based, run in the browser, and never see Hidden Pieces

Bots are not machine-learned. Each Bot samples plausible arrangements of the opponent's Hidden Pieces consistent with everything it has observed (Reveals, which Pieces have moved, the Graveyard), then searches a few Moves ahead in each sample and picks the Move that scores best on average. Difficulty comes from search depth, how many Attacks in a row are still followed past that depth, number of samples, how many positions one turn may search, deliberate random mistakes, and its Strategy. No Difficulty ever reads the true positions of Hidden Pieces. This is much simpler than training a model, runs in the player's browser so Bot games cost nothing and work without the server, and keeps Bots fair. Machine learning can be revisited if Hard proves too weak.

Hard chooses an Aggressive, Balanced or Defensive Strategy during Setup and keeps it throughout that game. The caller's Setup random generator chooses the Strategy so Setups can be replayed. Every Strategy puts the King on the front line in a lane open to the middle rows, and keeps the Princess on a random Home Row Square. Aggressive uses one adjacent Monster guard and favours advancing its Army, especially its King. Balanced uses two Monster guards and combines advancement with protecting the Princess. Defensive fills every Square next to the Princess with Monsters, using two guards in a corner or three elsewhere, and gives more weight to keeping friendly Pieces beside the Princess. Remaining Monsters stay away from the Princess's neighbouring Squares. Easy and Medium keep their Random Setups and original evaluation.

Strategies compose Setup Plans and add positional preferences through the PositionEvaluator interface. Preferences apply to the Bot's own Army, with their sign reversed when the search evaluates the opponent's turn. Advancing the Bot's Princess has less positional value in Aggressive play and no positional value in Balanced or Defensive play, so developing the Army takes priority over an unsupported Princess march. Enemy Princess advancement keeps its original threat value in every Strategy. Past the main search depth, tactical extensions follow both Attacks and immediate Princess wins, including quiet finishes on the enemy Home Row. An immediate Princess win still takes priority over every positional preference. The injected evaluator, legal Moves, terminal win and loss scores, and hidden-information boundary remain in force for every Strategy.
